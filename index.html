City cheated.
To catch up.

Manchester City disguised £830.7m of their owner's money as sponsorship. They should be punished. But they were chasing clubs with a huge head start, under rules that protected whoever was already ahead.

Commission findings published 29 September 2026. Sanctions to follow; City say they will appeal.

Before the cheating, the gap

When Abu Dhabi bought City in September 2008, United earned £278.5m a season. City earned £87m.

And the gap was growing, from £126m in 2002–03 to £191.5m in 2008–09. United built that lead legitimately, over decades.

Then came rules that locked it in

UEFA approved Financial Fair Play in September 2009, a year after the takeover. The Premier League followed with its own rules in 2013. Both limited spending to income plus a small allowance for losses.

Give two clubs the same £105m allowance and the richer one can still spend £600m more. Owners could no longer pay to close the gap. Chelsea's Roman Abramovich had done exactly that since 2003, before the rules existed.

The money was real. The label was false.

By late 2009 City feared breaking Chelsea's record £140m loss and failing the new rules. More money from the owner couldn't help, because it didn't count as income.

So in early 2010, the commission found, City began routing the owner's money through Abu Dhabi sponsors. Over nine seasons that came to £830.7m, or 29p in every £1 City reported earning.

It was deliberate. The commission found City "clearly intended to circumvent the PL Rules", that some witnesses were dishonest, and that the club obstructed the investigation.

The gap closed. It never shut.

The red area is owner money booked as sponsorship. It did much of the climbing.

Even so, City's reported revenue stayed below United's in every season covered by the charges. City cheated to chase the richest club in England, and still didn't catch it.

From sixth to second

Without the owner money, City's 2017–18 revenue of £368.8m was the lowest of the six biggest clubs. With it, City were second, behind United.

The clubs City passed, Liverpool, Chelsea, Arsenal and Spurs, paid for the cheating. United did not.

Now City have the head start

In 2008–09 United out-earned City 3.2 to 1. In 2024–25 City out-earned the average of the other 14 Premier League clubs 3.3 to 1.

The rules City broke now protect City.

Punish the cheat.
Fix the game.

Angry that City cheated? Good. Aim it at the rules too.

  • Let owners invest openly. Money paid in upfront and guaranteed, with nothing hidden.
  • Cap spending against the league, not your own past. Clubs voted down a cap tied to the bottom club's TV income, 7–12, in November 2025.
  • Share the money more fairly. Caps based on revenue turn today's income gap into a permanent one.

Method

  • Revenue figures are Deloitte's: the Football Money League to 2020–21 (latest restated figures), then the Annual Review of Football Finance. City's 2016–17 accounts cover 13 months.
  • Owner money booked as sponsorship is the commission's table of sums paid by ADUG, the owner's company, through Abu Dhabi sponsors (p. 20). Sponsor-by-sponsor figures are redacted.
  • "City without the owner money" subtracts only those sums. It is not a restatement of the accounts or an estimate of what City would have earned without its owner.
  • 29p is £830.69m divided by City's £2,830.9m of reported revenue from 2009–10 to 2017–18.
  • Other 14 average: 20 × league-average revenue, minus the six named clubs, divided by 14.
  • The rules chart uses hypothetical three-season incomes of £900m and £300m with the same £105m allowance.
